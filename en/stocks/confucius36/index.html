Detailed Explanation of the Lunyu: For All Those Who Misinterpret Confucius (36)

2006/11/30 11:38:31

The Master said: Without wisdom, one's mission cannot make one a gentleman; without wisdom, propriety cannot be established; without wisdom, speech cannot bring wisdom to people.

Yang Bojun: Confucius said: "Without understanding destiny, one cannot become a gentleman; without understanding propriety, one cannot establish oneself in society; without the ability to distinguish others' words, one cannot know people."
Qian Mu: The Master said: "Without understanding destiny, there's no way to be a gentleman. Without understanding the ritual system, there's no way to establish oneself. Without understanding language, there's no way to judge people."
Li Zehou: Confucius said: "Without knowing destiny, one cannot be a gentleman. Without knowing propriety, one cannot stand among people. Without knowing speech, one cannot know people."

Detailed Explanation: The above interpretations are all based on the following punctuation: "Without knowing destiny, one cannot be a gentleman. Without knowing propriety, one cannot establish oneself. Without knowing speech, one cannot know people." — and correspondingly maintain the absurd logic of "self-centrism." Only here the logical relationships are disguised as "destiny, propriety, speech," and the assumption is that once one possesses the so-called ability to know these "destiny, propriety, speech," one can receive the big red envelopes of "being a gentleman, establishing oneself, knowing people." The absurdity of this logic has been extensively discussed previously and need not be repeated.

The correct punctuation should be: "Without wisdom, one's mission cannot make one a gentleman; without wisdom, propriety cannot be established; without wisdom, speech cannot bring wisdom to people." What is discussed here are the various consequences of "not knowing" — of lacking wisdom. "Zhī" (知) is the original character of "zhì" (智, wisdom). "Not knowing" (不知) means lacking wisdom. The "not knowing" here refers not only to the individual person but also to the society of "people not knowing" itself. "Destiny/mission, propriety, speech" — none of these are purely individual phenomena. In the socialized existence of humanity, all are related to society itself. Unfortunately, under two thousand years of rotten Confucian meddling, these have been treated as empty-headed abstractions, as something a priori, completely violating the Confucian fundamental stance of grounding itself in reality.

"Mìng" (命) — mission. Even the Daoists can say "My fate is determined by me, not by heaven" — let alone the Confucians. In Chinese culture, the view of fate and destiny has always been far more positive and powerful than the Western tradition represented by ancient Greek tragedy. For the individual, "My fate is determined by me, not by heaven," thus "mìng" is merely one's mission — a mission not assigned by God, but coming from oneself. Heaven and earth are one's stage. Without wisdom, one cannot understand this "My fate is determined by me, not by heaven" — one believes that one's destiny is arranged by heaven, that slaves can only be slaves and rulers can only be rulers. Yet one doesn't know that all of this is "made by people." Only by breaking through this nonsense about heavenly mandate and destiny can one truly take on the real mission of being human. Only thus can one become a genuine gentleman capable of practicing "the Way of the Sage."

"Lǐ" (礼) — propriety, the most basic normal social order constituted by the various bottom lines of human relationships in society. If there are no minimum bottom lines between people in society, then society can only be a heap of loose sand, yielding only to various forms of violence and law. Violence and law are only needed when society cannot function normally. In a normal society, violence and law are last resorts. Yet people without wisdom, a society without wisdom, believe only in violence and law, abandoning all bottom lines. Thus, "propriety" cannot be established, the system connecting human relationships in society breaks apart, and society can only exist in turmoil.

"Yán" (言) — speech, discourse, including all speech and public discourse in social reality. A person without wisdom, a society without wisdom — their speech is insufficient to make people wise, and speech without wisdom cannot produce wisdom; it can only continue to create confusion and folly. Speech and action are closely related. Especially in a society with developed speech and media, the shaping effect of "speech" on people and on society cannot be overestimated.

Chán Zhōng Shuō Chán's Vernacular Translation

The Master said: Without wisdom, one's mission cannot make one a gentleman. Without wisdom, propriety cannot be established. Without wisdom, speech cannot bring wisdom to people.

Confucius said: Without wisdom, it is impossible to undertake the mission of a gentleman; without wisdom, it is impossible to establish normal social order; without wisdom, it is impossible to produce speech that brings wisdom to people.
