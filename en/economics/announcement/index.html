Your question isn't very clearly expressed. Generally, in this ID's terminology, 30-minute divergence creates at least a first-type sell point of the 30-minute level. If this 30-minute level sell point happens to be in a divergence segment on the daily chart, then it's simultaneously a first-type sell point on the daily chart.

After divergence occurs, first there will be a downward movement of at least 5-minute level, then a rebound—an upward movement of 5-minute level. Note: these movements don't have to be trends; consolidation works too. The divergence of the second 5-minute movement constitutes the second-type sell point.

The third-type sell point generally forms when no immediate decline occurs but a consolidation forms instead, and finally the consolidation can't hold, prices break below the hub, and a sub-level rebound fails to re-enter the hub.

Therefore, for selling, it's best to catch the divergence during the rally. The technical requirements for this are of course very high, but the efficiency and returns are also the highest. To achieve this, one must engage in arduous study and practice—there are no shortcuts.
