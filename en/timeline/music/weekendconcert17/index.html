2006/11/18 11:40:39

Whether he is the greatest in the history of music -- only one old man and another eternally young old man would have the standing to object. These three old men who spoke the same mother tongue: a brook deeper and vaster than the ocean, a song purer than any angel's aria, a spirit more powerful than the world itself -- the three true pillars of classical music's firmament, stars scattered among them, and from that point on the world became the world.

He was nothing but an incestuous man, a beast who ravaged his own nephew. In how many Viennese mornings and noons, incestuous moans pierced the depths of time, darkness seething like semen -- so said the moralists manufactured by semen themselves. Yet that spirit more powerful than the world itself, with five slender strings that pierced through the world, traced out the eternal trajectories of seething semen, and from that point on the world became spirit.

He, a deaf man -- the clamor of morality could not drown out this greatest of incestuous men. Every morning and noon, his pen, passing through the searing heat of a young body, ignited the deepest energies of the universe, transforming them into wave after wave of thunder. Notes and semen erupted together, bursting forth into sun, moon, and stars; mountains grew in convulsions, streams fell in ethereal grace -- a single feather across ten thousand ages of sky, and from that point on the world became resplendent.
