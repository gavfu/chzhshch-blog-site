Teaching You to Trade Stocks 11: Without Knowing How to Kiss, There Can Be No Climax!

2006/11/29 12:00:00

To identify "premature ejaculators," you must select three independent systems. One of the most commonly used is the so-called technical analysis toolkit. Pure technical analysis alone won't work, and pure non-technical analysis alone won't work either. Technical analysis tools can only achieve their full power when they're part of a three-independent-system framework.

The core idea of technical analysis is classification — a point that almost everyone who does technical analysis fails to understand. When a technical indicator issues a buy signal, the technical analyst treats it as if God has given a hint. With such a mindset, it's nearly impossible for any technical analyst to achieve great success. Technical indicators merely perform a complete classification of all possible market movements. Why are technical analysts all geniuses in hindsight but go limp when it's time to act? This is precisely the reason.

There's too much to say about technical analysis — this indicator and that indicator, how to apply them. The key is the classification problem discussed above. Any technical indicator merely classifies the market completely from that indicator's perspective and points out what's playable and what's not playable. As for whether the conditions corresponding to this indicator are 100% reflected in actual price movements — the answer is certainly no. Otherwise, everyone could just trade according to this indicator, and who would lose money? However, if you examine technical indicators purely from the standpoint of classification, then technical indicators will unleash their maximum power.

The simplest yet most practical technical indicator system is the so-called moving average system. The moving average system is obviously not a very precise system — there are too many fakeouts. If you buy whenever price breaks above a certain moving average and sell when it breaks below, your success rate will definitely not be high, especially when that moving average is short-term. What's truly useful is the moving average system — that is, a technical evaluation system composed of several moving averages representing short, medium, and long-term trends. Note: any technical indicator or system is essentially an evaluation system that tells you the strength or weakness of the evaluated object under that system's standards. For example, with a single 5-day moving average, standing above it means that using the 5-day moving average to classify all market situations, the current situation of being above the 5-day MA indicates strength. However, while standing above the 5-day MA, price might be below the 10-day MA. Under the 10-day MA system's evaluation, this situation would be weakness. So is the corresponding trend strong or weak?

In fact, strength and weakness are all relative — what matters is the standard by which you enter. For ultra-ultra-short-term traders, showing strength on a 1-minute chart is enough to enter, especially under T+0 conditions where such operations are perfectly normal. But for large funds, even 5-day strength on the daily chart isn't enough to pique their interest. The primary selection criteria for any technical indicator system's application are always related to the capital size and operational timeframe involved. Without these considerations, any further discussion is meaningless. Therefore, everyone should consider how to select appropriate parameters based on their own actual circumstances. Once you understand the underlying principles, the application is entirely up to your own judgment.

With moving average systems, there are inevitably questions about the relationships between the various moving averages. The relationship between any two moving averages is essentially a matter of "kissing." Using the kissing standard, the corresponding relationships can be completely classified: flying kiss, lip kiss, and wet kiss. Think of the short-term moving average as the queen and the long-term moving average as the gigolo. Then "male-on-top" signifies a bear market, while "female-on-top" signifies a bull market. To make money, you want more "female-on-top" positions.

Flying kiss: The short-term moving average briefly flattens, then continues in its original trend direction.
Lip kiss: The short-term moving average approaches the long-term moving average without breaking below or above it, then continues in its original trend direction.
Wet kiss: The short-term moving average breaks below or above the long-term moving average, sometimes even entangling repeatedly, intertwined like lovers.

Flying kisses occur relatively rarely — generally only when the trend is particularly strong. However, excessively intense trends can't last long, so subsequent oscillation often follows. Lip kisses are the most commonly seen pattern during any basic trend process, especially during "male-on-top" situations where it's almost always this pattern. Once a lip kiss bounce occurs in a bear market, the rebound is basically over. In "female-on-top" situations, the probability of an adjustment ending is also quite high, though you must guard against a lip kiss evolving into a wet kiss. Wet kisses — these are commonly seen during significant corrections after a trend, and also when trends are reversing. This is especially true during "male-on-top" situations: if short, medium, and long-term moving averages all come together for an NP wet kiss, such a pornographic AV scene often signals that a major trend reversal is imminent — the skies are about to change, and "male-on-top" will become "female-on-top."

Note: trend reversals almost always have a high probability of being triggered by wet kisses. There are two situations here: one is a wet kiss followed by one big climax in the original trend direction, creating a trap, then reversing; the other is repeated wet kisses constructing a reversal-type box, after which the climax signals the change in position. In "male-on-top" situations, once a wet kiss appears, pay close attention, especially if this wet kiss comes after a long-term "male-on-top" period — pay even more attention. The subsequent drop is often an excellent entry opportunity, because the probability of it being a bear trap is enormous. I must note: this does not apply to the first wet kiss after a trend is formed. But after a wet kiss, there must be a climax — the only difference is the position. The key judgment is about the position, not whether a climax will occur.

Know how to kiss, and there will be climax. If you can't even kiss, how can there be any climax?
